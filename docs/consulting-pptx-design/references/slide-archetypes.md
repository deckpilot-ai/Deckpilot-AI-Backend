# Slide Archetypes

Twelve recurring layouts observed across the source decks. They apply to any subject —
educational chapters, business reviews, product pitches, research summaries. A 15-30 slide
deck typically uses the first two once each, the middle ones repeated and varied across the
bulk of the deck, and the last two once each at the end. **Never place two of the same
archetype back to back** — vary the visual rhythm the way the source decks do.

## 1. Title slide (1 per deck, opens the deck)

Dark full-bleed background (primary color). Structure, top to bottom:
- Small colored "chip" rect: `CHAPTER N`, `SECTION N`, or a program/client line — whatever frames this deck.
- Deck title, white, large (36-44pt Cambria bold), can span two lines.
- Subtitle line in a muted tint bar underneath.
- Row of 3 small pill chips naming the deck's major sections/themes.
- A thin bottom accent: small icon circle + one-line description of the deck's throughline.
- 2-3 oversized low-alpha decorative ellipses bleeding off the corners (see design-system.md).
- Some decks (Understanding Markets) instead use a real full-width illustrative photo strip across the top with the title below — either treatment is valid; pick photo-banner if you have a strong hero image from the source material, decorative-ellipses if you don't.

## 2. Roadmap / agenda / "Big Questions" (1 per deck, slide 2)

Light background. Either:
- 3-4 numbered question cards in a row (badge number in a colored circle, question text below), title "The Big Questions"; or
- A vertical list of section rows, each with an icon, section title, one-line description, and a number badge on the right — title "What This Deck Covers" / "Agenda" / "Chapter Roadmap," whichever fits the deck's framing.

Both versions exist in this project; pick whichever matches how granular the deck's sections are (use the question format for concept-driven material, the roadmap-list format for material with many discrete named sections — this works the same for a business deck's agenda slide as it does for a textbook chapter's roadmap).

## 3. Definition / concept-anchor slide

Eyebrow + title + 1-2 paragraphs of body text on the left, paired with either a real photo
(right half) or a small paired-contrast card set (e.g. NEEDS vs WANTS, two labeled boxes
with an icon circle between them). Use early in the deck to establish a core term or
concept before building on it — a business deck might use this to define a market segment
or a KPI the same way an educational deck defines a vocabulary term.

## 4. Two-column comparison

Two side-by-side cards of equal width, each with its own header (often colored differently
per side, e.g. one card teal-headed, one red-headed, for a "system A vs system B"
comparison), 3-4 bullet rows each. Use for any "X vs Y" contrast in the source material —
federal vs unitary government, physical vs online markets, renewable vs non-renewable, or
just as easily "build vs buy," "this quarter vs last," or "plan A vs plan B" in a business deck.

## 5. Icon + text card grid (2x2, 2x3, or 3x1)

A grid of equal cards, each with an icon in a colored circle, a bold short header, and a
one-to-two-line description. This is the single most-reused archetype in the project —
vary the grid shape (2x2, 3-across, 2x3) and the icon set each time it recurs so the deck
doesn't feel repetitive.

## 6. Stat callout row

2-4 large-number cards side by side (see design-system.md's stat callout spec), each
number paired with a short caption. Use whenever the source material has genuine
extractable figures — never invent numbers to fill this layout.

## 7. Process flow / numbered steps

A horizontal (or occasionally vertical) sequence of 3-4 numbered nodes connected by
arrows or chevrons, each node a small card or icon+label. Use for "how X is formed" or
"the journey of goods" type sequential content (e.g. the market-chain, the route to
federation).

## 8. Timeline / table

Either a 3-column table (era headers, e.g. "Foundation / Struggle and expansion / Recovery
and decline") with rows of year+event pairs, or a horizontal timeline of dated milestones.
Use for chronological content.

## 9. Case study with real image

Eyebrow labeled `CASE STUDY · [specific example]`, a real photo or diagram if the source
material has one (often a strip of 2-4 related images), a short narrative paragraph, and
usually one supporting stat or quote card. This is where extracted real photos matter most
— see image-pipeline.md. Never substitute an AI-generated image for a real one when a real
one exists; if no real image exists for this example, use a supporting icon/illustration
instead of faking a photo.

## 10. Quote / synthesis slide

Can be light (cream/tinted quote card on a white background) or full-dark (quote card on
the primary color, used for the deck's emotional or thesis-level statement). Bold italic
quote, attribution line below. Use sparingly — once or twice per deck, at moments that earn it.

## 11. Dark conceptual panel

A full-dark-background slide (not just a quote) used for a slide-level concept that benefits
from visual weight — e.g. "Government's Role in the Market: Four Levers," "The
Sustainability of Development." Structure: eyebrow (in accent-tinted color), white title, a
grid of cards using the dark-card treatment from design-system.md (tinted-primary card fill,
white headers, light-tint body text, numbered/icon badges). Use once or twice per deck for
a top-level synthesizing idea, not for routine content.

## 12. Closing slides: Key Takeaways + final dark slide

- **Key Takeaways** (second-to-last): light background, a grid of 4-6 short takeaway cards, each with a small icon and one punchy sentence — no new information, only synthesis of what's already been covered.
- **Final slide**: mirrors the title slide's dark treatment (same decorative ellipses, same primary color) with a short closing line or open question ("Where do we want to go?") and the deck's name/breadcrumb repeated for a bookend feel.

## Sequencing guidance

A typical 20-25 slide deck: title (1) → roadmap (1) → concept-anchor (2-3) →
mix of comparison / icon-grid / stat / process-flow / timeline / case-study archetypes,
varied slide to slide (14-17 slides) → 1-2 dark conceptual panels placed where the
deck's argument needs a beat of emphasis (not necessarily centered in the deck) →
key takeaways (1) → final dark slide (1). Shorter business decks (10-15 slides) can drop
the dark conceptual panel and compress the content-slide mix, keeping the same title →
roadmap → content → takeaways → closing skeleton.
